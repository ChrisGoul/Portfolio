Pressure vessel, high-temperature thermal system, custom instrumentation, and
the sample assemblies that go inside it.

The compression stage is a conventional Kawai-type multi-anvil arrangement:
eight tungsten carbide cubes, corners truncated, closing on an octahedral
pressure medium with pyrophyllite gaskets that extrude and seal as the assembly
compresses. That geometry is decades old and well documented — nothing novel
about it. The press driving it and the cell design are my own and stay
undocumented here.

## What comes out

Products are broken out of the recovered cell, cleaned up and picked through
under a stereo microscope. Identification at this stage is by eye — habit, lustre
and hardness — so the crystals below are promising rather than confirmed until
they go through proper analysis.

## Where it started

The press began as an attempt at synthetic marble. Marble is a metamorphic rock —
a matrix of large-grained crystals that reads as translucent. Commercial
synthetics are crushed powder and rock in a plastic or clay binder, which
approximates the surface appearance but not the translucency. Reproducing the
geologic pressures and temperatures that metamorphose limestone gets you material
that matches optically.

Same pressure-vessel problem as the diamond work, an order of magnitude lower in
pressure — which made it a good place to learn on.

Stacked plates give you one thing a pneumatic cylinder does not: constant force.
Air compresses, so as a piston travels its pressure rises and the load the user
feels climbs through the rep. Each version of this machine is a different answer
to that problem.

## Three attempts at constant force

**V1** — a large air tank. Make the reservoir big enough relative to the swept
volume and the pressure rise over a stroke becomes small. It works, and it is
bulky.

<figure class="full">
<img src="{{ '/assets/V1.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Version 1 of the pneumatic weight machine" loading="lazy">
</figure>

**V2** — a mechanical fix instead. A sprocket with a changing radius winds the
cable, so as pressure rises the moment arm shrinks and the cable tension stays
flat. Proof of concept, less polished.

<figure class="full">
<img src="{{ '/assets/V2_Machine.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Version 2, using a variable-radius sprocket" loading="lazy">
</figure>

**V3** — closed-loop control. Measure cable tension, regulate pressure against
it, and the profile becomes something you set rather than something the geometry
gives you. Compact, portable, adjustable, with a digital force readout.

Materials · High pressure

HPHT Cubic Press

A laboratory press built to reach the multi-GPa, high-temperature conditions where diamond is the stable phase of carbon — pressure vessel, high-temperature thermal system, custom instrumentation and the sample assemblies that go inside it.

The compression stage is a conventional Kawai-type multi-anvil arrangement: eight tungsten carbide cubes, corners truncated, closing on an octahedral pressure medium with pyrophyllite gaskets that extrude and seal as the assembly compresses. That geometry is decades old and well documented — nothing novel about it. The press driving it and the cell design are my own and stay undocumented here.

Recovered material

Products are broken out of the recovered cell, cleaned up and picked through under a stereo microscope. Identification is by eye at this stage — habit, lustre and hardness — so the crystals below are promising rather than confirmed until they go through proper analysis.

Where it started: synthetic marble

The press began as an attempt at synthetic marble. Marble is a metamorphic rock — a matrix of large-grained crystals that reads as translucent. Commercial synthetics are crushed powder and rock held in a plastic or clay binder, which approximates the surface appearance but not the translucency.

Reproducing the geologic pressures and temperatures that metamorphose limestone gets you material that matches optically. It is the same pressure-vessel problem as the diamond work, an order of magnitude lower in pressure — which made it a good place to learn on.
